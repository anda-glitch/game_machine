How the odds are computed

  1. Read the numbers. Each revealed number says how many mines sit among its hidden neighbours.
  2. Plain logic first. A number whose mines are all accounted for makes its other neighbours safe; a number with exactly as many hidden neighbours as mines makes them all mines. Comparing overlapping numbers (the subset rule) settles more.
  3. Count every layout. The remaining frontier squares split into independent groups. For each group the solver lists every mine arrangement that satisfies all its numbers.
  4. Weight by the rest of the board. An arrangement using m mines leaves the other mines to fall anywhere in the unexplored area, which they can do in C(U, M − m) ways. Arrangements that leave a "likely" number of mines for the open sea count for more.
  5. Probability = share of weighted layouts with a mine on that square. These are exact; a brute-force check agrees to 15 decimal places.
